Who you’d be dealing with

01 “Who are you?” A small team of university students.

The company is Tooey LLC. We're early: Tooey is in a pilot with independent restaurants, and the people who answer your email are the same people building your forecast.

02 “Students. Will you still be around in a year?” We can't promise that.

So here's what you'd lose if we weren't. Tooey works from a copy of your sales, so your POS and the way you prep stay as they are. If we disappeared, you'd lose a morning email, not your records.

What arrives, and how far to trust it

03 “What exactly do I get?” One list. That's all of it, for now.

Every dish gets two numbers: how many you'll likely sell, and how many to make. Beside them is the change from the same day last week, and the dishes are grouped by category. Print it and keep it on the line.

The example at the top of this page is what it looks like, down to the blanks.

04 “Is this another AI thing?” There's nothing to chat with.

There's a forecasting model behind the list, built on your own sales. You won't type questions into anything or learn a new screen. You read a list, the way you'd read one your chef wrote.

05 “Where does the number come from?” Your own sales, and the calendar.

Each night Tooey takes the day's sales and works out the coming day for each dish from four things.

  • Your past sales, dish by dish
  • Day of the week
  • Time of year
  • Holidays
  • Weather
  • Local events
  • Reservations

The dashed three aren't used today. They may be one day.

06 “Will the count be right?” Not every day.

It's a forecast, and kitchens have days nobody saw coming. Treat the count as a starting point, not an order. You know about the bus tour booked for Friday and the road closed out front; the list doesn't. You still make the call.

07 “So how accurate is it?” We haven't measured that at a live restaurant yet.

So you won't find an accuracy figure, a savings figure, or a percentage of any kind on this site. During the pilot you hold the list up against what you really sold and judge it yourself, before you rely on it.

08 “What about dishes you can't predict?” They stay blank, with the reason.

A dish that's been on the menu less than about five weeks, or sells under about one a day, gets no number rather than a guess. The blank means we declined. It doesn't mean make zero.

What it takes from you

09 “How much of my time does it take?” About a week of setup. Then a list to read.

  1. BeforeAbout five weeks of past salesAlready in your POS, if you've used it that long.
  2. Setup, about a weekYou tell us what matters in your kitchenYou choose how the list is laid out, then check it before you rely on it.
  3. After thatAn email each morning before prepPrint it if you like, and prep from it.

10 “Will anyone have to type in sales?” No.

We set up a nightly download from your POS. It works with most systems: Square, Toast, and Clover sales exports are recognized as they come, and other exports can be mapped. Tell us what you use and we'll tell you plainly if it's a problem.

11 “What do you do with my sales?” Forecast your restaurant. Nothing else.

We don't sell your sales, share them with other restaurants, or publish them. The only people who see them are the Tooey team members building your forecast.

The rest is on the privacy page, in the same plain terms.

The catch, and the way out

12 “It's free. What's the catch?” It's early. That's the catch.

Tooey is free for now because we're in a pilot, which means you'd be one of the first kitchens using it. We haven't set a price or a trial length, so you won't find either here, and we won't make one up when we talk.

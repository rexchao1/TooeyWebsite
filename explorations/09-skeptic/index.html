Answers

01“Who are you?”

A small team of university students.

The company is Tooey LLC. We're early: Tooey is in a pilot with independent restaurants, and the people who answer your email are the same people building your forecast.

02“Students. Will you still be around in a year?”

We can't promise that.

So here's what you'd lose if we weren't. Tooey works from a copy of your sales, so your POS and the way you prep stay as they are. If we disappeared, you'd lose a morning email, not your records.

03“What exactly do I get?”

One list. That's all of it, for now.

Every dish gets two numbers: how many you'll likely sell, and how many to make. Beside them is the change from the same day last week, and the dishes are grouped by category. Print it and keep it on the line.

A dish that's been on the menu less than about five weeks, or sells under about one a day, stays blank with the reason instead of getting a guess. A blank means we declined. It doesn't mean make zero.

The sample near the top shows both.

04“Is this another AI thing?”

There's nothing to chat with.

There's a forecasting model behind the list. Each night it takes the day's sales and works out the coming day, dish by dish, from four things:

  • Your past sales
  • Day of the week
  • Time of year
  • Holidays
“Does it know about the weather?”
Not yet.
“Local events?”
Not yet.
“Reservations?”
Not yet.

You won't type questions into anything or learn a new screen. You read a list, the way you'd read one your chef wrote.

05“Will the count be right?”

Not every day, and we haven't measured how often.

Nobody has checked it at a live restaurant yet, so there's no accuracy figure, savings figure, or percentage anywhere on this site. Treat the count as a starting point, not an order. You know about the bus tour booked for Friday and the road closed out front; the list doesn't.

During the pilot you hold the list up against what you really sold, and you decide whether to rely on it.

06“How much of my time does it take?”

About a week of setup. Nobody types in sales.

  1. About five weeksof past sales to start, already in your POS if you've used it that long.
  2. About a weekof setup. You tell us what matters in your kitchen, choose how the list is laid out, and check it before you rely on it.
  3. Then each morningan email before prep.

We set up a nightly download from your POS. It works with most systems: Square, Toast, and Clover sales exports are recognized as they come, and other exports can be mapped. Tell us what you use and we'll tell you plainly if it's a problem.

07“What do you do with my sales?”

Forecast your restaurant. Nothing else.

We don't sell your sales, share them with other restaurants, or publish them. The only people who see them are the Tooey team members building your forecast.

The rest is on the privacy page, in the same plain terms.

08“It’s free. What’s the catch?”

It's early. That's the catch.

Tooey is free for now because we're in a pilot, which means you'd be one of the first kitchens using it. We haven't set a price or a trial length, so you won't find either here, and we won't make one up when we talk.

We're building toward one place where an owner can see what the restaurant's own numbers mean and what to do about them. Prep came first because it happens every day and your POS already holds what it needs.

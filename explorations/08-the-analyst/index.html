Every night, Tooey goes through your sales the way a restaurant group’s analyst would.

It starts with the call you make every morning before prep: how many of each dish to make. Tooey works that out overnight from your own sales and emails you the list.

Join the pilot What happens overnight

At an independent restaurant, nobody’s job is reading the numbers.

At a restaurant group, someone’s is. They go through what sold, hold it up against the same week last year and the holiday coming up, and tell each kitchen what to expect. They work out which plates pay for themselves and how many people each shift needs.

At an independent, the person who would do that is also placing orders, writing the schedule, getting the walk-in fixed, and working the pass. A lot of the answers are already sitting in the POS. There aren’t enough hours in the day to dig them out.

Tooey is on the independent restaurant’s side of that gap. We’re building software to do that work for one restaurant at a time, every night.

Prep is the first part of that job we’ve taken on.

It comes first because a wrong guess costs you every single day. Make too much and it ends up in the bin; make too little and you’re 86ing it by eight. The rest of this list is where Tooey is going. We aren’t putting dates on it.

What happens between close and prep

You don’t do anything at night. A nightly download, which we set up with you, brings the day’s sales over from your POS, and the rest runs on its own. Nobody at Tooey sits up going through your numbers by hand. People come in at setup, and whenever you write to us.

  1. After close

    The day’s sales come over.

    Item by item, from your POS. The download works with most systems. Square, Toast, and Clover sales exports are read as they are, and others can be mapped.

  2. Overnight

    Every count is worked out again.

    For each dish, Tooey weighs your own past sales against the day of the week, the time of year, and holidays, then sets Predicted and Prep for the coming day.

  3. Before prep

    The list is in your inbox.

    Grouped by category, laid out the way you chose at setup. Print it and use it on the line.

  4. The next night

    It starts again with one more day.

    Each day’s sales go into the next night’s counts, so the list keeps up with your menu and your season.

What goes into a count

Used today

  • Your past item-level sales
  • Day of the week
  • Time of year
  • Holidays

Not yet

  • Weather
  • Local events
  • Reservations

Those three are where the forecasting part of the job goes next. Today’s counts don’t know about them, so a rained-out patio or a concert down the street is still your call.

A blank is a decline, not a zero.

Burrata New: three weeks on the menu

When a dish has been on the menu less than about five weeks, or sells under about one a day, there isn’t enough behind it for an honest count. Tooey leaves that row blank and prints the reason, instead of handing you a number it can’t stand behind. Make your usual call on that one.

The counts it does give are still forecasts. We haven’t measured accuracy at a live restaurant yet, so you won’t find an accuracy figure on this site. During the pilot you check each count against what actually sold, and you decide how far to trust it.

A prep count can miss in two directions, and only one of them leaves a trace.

Example: 16 prepped, 13 sold, 3 in the bin

Too many

The extras go to staff meal, tomorrow’s special, or the bin. Either way you paid for every ingredient in them, and it lands in your food cost whether anyone wrote it down or not.

Each one costs youits plate cost
Example: 10 prepped, 10 sold, 4 asked for after it ran out

Too few

The dish is 86’d by quarter past eight, and the tables after that order something else, or something cheaper. Your POS records what you sold, not what you would have sold, so this one never shows up on a report.

Each one costs youup to its price, less its plate cost

“Up to”, because a guest who can’t get the dish may order something else instead. Then you lose only the difference, if any.

Put the two side by side and a rule falls out of the arithmetic. Whenever a dish sells for more than twice what it costs to plate, a missed order can cost you more than a portion in the bin. It’s why the Prep number sits a little above Predicted: so a rush doesn’t leave you short.

What happens between close and the next morning.

  1. After close

    The day’s sales come in from your POS. We set up the nightly download once, during setup, and it runs on its own after that.

  2. Overnight

    Every dish is worked out again for the coming day: how many you’re likely to sell, and how many to make.

  3. Before prep

    The list is in your email, grouped by category. Print it and keep it on the line.

The download works with most POS systems. Square, Toast and Clover sales exports are recognized, and others can be mapped.

What goes into a count

Four things, all of them yours or the calendar’s.

  • Your past salesDish by dish
  • The day of the weekA Tuesday is not a Saturday
  • The time of yearPatio season, slow months
  • HolidaysThe ones that fill you or empty you

Not in the count yet: weather, local events, reservations. We want to add them later.

When a line is left blank

Some dishes don’t have enough history to count from: anything on the menu for less than about five weeks, or that sells less than about one a day. Tooey leaves those lines blank and says why, rather than make up a number. A blank isn’t a zero. It means that one is your call.

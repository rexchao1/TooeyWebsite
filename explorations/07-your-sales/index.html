What your sales already know

You know Saturdays are busy. Your sales know how busy, for each dish, and how that changes in January, in July, and the week of Thanksgiving.

Here is one dish at a sample restaurant: a year of nights, one number each, the way a POS keeps them. The readings below add nothing to it. They only change which numbers stand out.

  1. The day of the week

    Friday and Saturday nights sell about 33 plates. Mondays and Tuesdays sell about 15. That holds all year.

  2. The time of year

    It’s a cold-weather dish. A Saturday in January sells about 41. A Saturday in July sells about 26.

  3. Holidays

    Some nights ignore the usual week. Valentine’s Day sold 60, on a Saturday that usually does about 40. The Fourth of July, also a Saturday, sold 15.

  4. Each dish’s own sales

    Fish tacos tell a different story: about 7 a night last October, about 16 now. Each dish has its own record, and each gets its own count.

Nobody running a restaurant has time to read a year like this for every dish, every night. Tooey does, and it all ends up in one place: the count next to each dish on your morning list. What goes in is the day of the week, the time of year, holidays, and each dish’s own sales. Weather, local events, and reservations don’t.

What arrives in the morning

An email before prep, with every dish on your menu grouped the way you set it up. Each dish gets two numbers: how many we expect you to sell and how many to make. Print it and take it to the line.

During the pilot, you check our counts against what actually sold before you rely on them. What goes on the line stays your call.

What it takes to start

  1. Five weeks of sales

    Tooey needs about five weeks of past sales to start. If your POS has been running that long, you have them.

  2. A nightly download

    We set up a download from your POS each night. It works with most systems: Square, Toast, and Clover sales exports are read as they are, and we can map others.

  3. About a week of setup

    You tell us what matters in your kitchen and choose how your list is laid out. Then you check it before you rely on it.

Free for now, while we’re in a pilot.

What we collect, and why

The same record holds more

Today Tooey does one thing: the morning prep count. These are the questions we want to answer from the same sales next. None of them is built yet.

  • Which dishes actually make money

    Your sales already say what sells. Set next to what each plate costs, they would say what pays.

  • How many people to put on

    A busy Saturday shows up in your sales history long before it walks in the door. A schedule could be built around it.

  • When to bring guests back

    Slow stretches come back at the same time each year. That’s when a reason to come in counts most.

  • More than your sales

    Rain, a game downtown, a street fair. The count doesn’t use any of them today. We’d like it to.

How Tooey works

Start with sales. Don't change the kitchen. Decide if the numbers are any good.

  1. You send sales history.

    Item-level, not just the day's total. A spreadsheet is fine. Covers and other demand signals help if you have them. They aren't required to start.

  2. Tooey writes a week of forecasts.

    Each menu item gets a count for the days ahead, plus a range. You prep and buy the way you already do. Tooey sits next to that week. It doesn't run it.

  3. You compare it to what actually sold.

    If the counts are close enough on your menu, you say so. If they aren't, we don't go further. You decide what close enough means.

  4. Prep and purchasing only if you want them.

    Recipes turn a count into a prep list. Inventory and vendors turn that into an order. We don't need those to forecast, and we don't ask until the counts have earned it.

What we need from you

  • Item-level sales history. A spreadsheet is enough.
  • A week where the kitchen runs the way it already runs.
  • Someone who will look at the forecast and say whether it's any good.

What you get that week

  • A count for each item, for the days ahead.
  • A range around that count.
  • Nothing else in the way. No new process while you judge the numbers.

What can come later

If the week holds up, you can share recipes, ingredient quantities, inventory, and how you buy. Then the same forecast can turn into prep and purchasing, not only portions of each dish. You don't need any of that for the first week.

We're doing this with a couple of restaurants. It's free for them, for a little while.

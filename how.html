How Tooey works

Start with sales. Don't change the kitchen. Decide if the numbers are any good.

  1. You send sales history.

    Item-level, not just the day's total. A spreadsheet is fine. A CSV. A dump from the POS. If you track covers, send those too. Other demand signals help when we have them. They are not required to start.

  2. Tooey writes a week of forecasts.

    Each menu item gets a count for the days ahead, plus a range, because a single number is never the whole truth. You run prep and purchasing the way you already do. Tooey sits next to that week, not inside it yet.

  3. You compare it to what actually sold.

    That comparison is the product, at the beginning. If the counts are close enough on your menu, you say so. If they aren't, we don't pretend they were. You decide what close enough means.

  4. Prep and purchasing only if you want them.

    Recipes and portions turn an item count into a prep list. Inventory and purchasing turn that into an order. We do not need those to forecast. We need them to help with the rest, and only after the forecast has earned that.

What we need from you

  • Item-level sales history. A spreadsheet is enough.
  • A week where the kitchen runs the way it already runs.
  • Someone who will look at the forecast and say whether it's any good.

What you get that week

  • A count for each item, for the days ahead.
  • A range around that count.
  • Nothing else in the way. No new process while you judge the numbers.

What can come later

If the week holds up, you can share recipes, ingredient quantities, inventory, and how you buy. Then the same forecast can speak in prep and purchasing, not only in portions of each dish. That step is yours to take. It is not the price of trying the first week.

We're doing this with a couple of restaurants. It's free for them, for a little while.
